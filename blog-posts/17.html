How to Stay Safe on Public WiFi: 9 Essential Tips

Free WiFi at a café in Lahore, a hotel in Karachi, or the airport while waiting for your flight — it’s convenient, but it’s also one of the easiest places for attackers to steal your data. On an open network, your traffic can be snooped, faked login pages can harvest your passwords, and a stranger’s laptop can quietly sit between you and the internet.

The good news: you don’t need to be a tech expert to protect yourself. These nine practical tips will keep you safe on any public network.

Why Public WiFi Is Risky

Most public hotspots are open networks — no password, no encryption. That means:

  • Anyone nearby can see unencrypted traffic using simple, freely available tools.
  • Evil twin attacks: an attacker creates a hotspot named “Cafe_Free_WiFi” that looks official; you connect to their device instead of the real router.
  • Man-in-the-middle attacks: even on password-protected hotel WiFi (where everyone shares the same password), another guest can intercept your traffic.
  • Session hijacking: on unencrypted sites, an attacker can steal your login session and impersonate you.

You can’t tell a safe network from a dangerous one just by looking at your phone. So treat every public network as untrustworthy and protect yourself accordingly.

Tip 1: Use a VPN on Public Networks

This is the single most effective step. A VPN encrypts everything leaving your device, so even if someone is snooping on the café WiFi, all they see is scrambled data.

  • Install a reputable VPN app before you travel (don’t wait until you’re on the risky network).
  • Connect the VPN before opening your bank app, email, or anything with a login.
  • Free plans from trustworthy providers (like Proton VPN’s unlimited free tier) are fine for occasional use — see our free VPN guide for options.

One tap, and the biggest public-WiFi risk disappears.

Tip 2: Make Sure Websites Use HTTPS

HTTPS encrypts the connection between you and a website. Look for the padlock icon in your browser’s address bar.

  • Never enter passwords, card numbers, or personal details on a site showing “Not Secure” or plain http://.
  • Modern browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Edge) warn you about insecure sites — don’t click through the warning.
  • Consider the HTTPS Everywhere-style protection: most browsers now have an “HTTPS-Only Mode” in settings — turn it on.

Note: HTTPS protects your data from the WiFi snoopers, but the network owner can still see which sites you visit. A VPN hides that too.

Tip 3: Forget the Network When You’re Done

Your phone loves to auto-reconnect to networks it has joined before. Attackers exploit this: your phone broadcasts “is CoffeeShop_WiFi here?” and their evil-twin hotspot answers “yes!”

After using public WiFi:

  • Android: Settings → Network & Internet → WiFi → Saved networks → tap the network → Forget.
  • iPhone: Settings → WiFi → tap the ⓘ next to the network → Forget This Network.
  • Turn off auto-join for public networks, and disable “Ask to join networks”/WiFi auto-connect when you’re out.

Tip 4: Turn Off Sharing and AirDrop

On laptops, public networks are full of strangers’ devices. Make sure yours isn’t advertising itself:

  • Windows: when connecting, choose the “Public” network profile (not Private). This turns off file/printer sharing. Check: Settings → Network → WiFi → network properties → set to Public.
  • Mac: System Settings → General → Sharing — turn off File Sharing, Printer Sharing, etc.
  • iPhone: set AirDrop to “Contacts Only” or “Receiving Off” (Settings → General → AirDrop).
  • Android: turn off Nearby Share visibility to everyone.

Tip 5: Use Your Phone’s Hotspot Instead

Your mobile data (Jazz, Zong, Telenor, Ufone) is far safer than café WiFi because the connection between your phone and the tower is encrypted and much harder to intercept.

  • If you need to do banking or sensitive work, tether from your phone instead of joining the public hotspot.
  • Watch your data usage — hotspot eats data faster than you expect. A quick mobile-banking session uses very little; video calls use a lot.
  • Set a strong hotspot password (Settings → Hotspot → WPA2/WPA3, not “Open”).

Tip 6: Keep Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) On

Even if someone steals your password on public WiFi, 2FA stops them from getting into your account.

  • Turn on 2FA for email, banking, Facebook, WhatsApp, and Google/Apple accounts — email is the most important, since password resets go there.
  • Prefer an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator) over SMS codes — SIM-swap fraud is a real problem in Pakistan.
  • Save backup codes somewhere safe (written down, not in your email).

Tip 7: Avoid Sensitive Transactions When Possible

Sometimes the safest move is simply waiting:

  • Banking, Easypaisa/JazzCash transfers, and online shopping are best done on your home WiFi or mobile data.
  • If you must do it on public WiFi, use the official bank app (not the browser), with a VPN connected, and HTTPS confirmed.
  • Never enter card details on a site you reached through a link someone sent you — type the bank’s address yourself.

Tip 8: Keep Your Devices Updated

Those annoying update notifications are security patches. Attackers on public networks often exploit known bugs in old software.

  • Turn on automatic updates for your phone’s OS and your apps.
  • Update your browser — Chrome/Firefox updates patch the exact vulnerabilities WiFi attackers use.
  • Don’t ignore the “update available” prompt for your banking apps.

Tip 9: Watch for Fake Login Pages (Captive Portals Done Right)

Hotels and malls often show a login/terms page when you join their WiFi (a “captive portal”). Attackers fake these too.

  • Only enter details the venue actually requires (usually just a room number or “Accept”).
  • Never enter your email password, bank login, or social media password into a WiFi login page. A real captive portal never asks for those.
  • If the portal looks glitchy or the URL looks odd, disconnect and ask the staff for the correct network name.

Bonus: What to Do If You Think You’ve Been Hacked on Public WiFi

Act fast, in this order:

  1. Disconnect from the WiFi immediately; switch to mobile data.
  2. Change passwords for email first, then banking, then social media — from a trusted network.
  3. Check bank/Easypaisa/JazzCash statements for unknown transactions and report them to your bank’s helpline right away.
  4. Log out of all sessions (Gmail: “Manage devices”; Facebook: “Where you’re logged in”).
  5. Run a malware scan on your device.

FAQ

Is hotel WiFi with a password safe? Safer than open WiFi, but not fully safe — everyone in the hotel shares that password, so another guest can still snoop. Use a VPN anyway.

Does “HTTPS” mean I’m 100% safe without a VPN? It protects the content of your browsing on that site, but not which sites you visit, and it doesn’t protect apps that don’t use encryption. A VPN covers everything. Use both.

Can the café owner see what I’m doing? On open WiFi without a VPN, yes — they can see which websites you visit and any unencrypted data. With a VPN, they only see encrypted traffic to the VPN server.

Should I use public WiFi for Easypaisa or JazzCash? Avoid it if you can. Use mobile data or wait until you’re home. If you must, use the official app with a VPN on.

Is it safe to charge my phone at public USB stations? That’s a different risk (“juice jacking”) — a compromised USB port can access your phone’s data. Use your own charger plugged into a wall socket, or a USB data-blocker adapter.

Conclusion

Public WiFi doesn’t have to be scary — it just demands a little discipline. The 30-second version: connect a VPN first, check for HTTPS, forget the network afterwards, and keep 2FA on everywhere. Do those four things and you’re safer than 95% of people on the same network.
