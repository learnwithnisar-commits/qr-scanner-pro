How to Speed Up a Slow Android Phone: 12 Proven Tips

Every Android phone slows down eventually. Apps take forever to open, the keyboard lags, and you start wondering if you need a new phone. Before you spend tens of thousands of rupees on an upgrade, try these 12 proven fixes — most take just a few minutes and cost nothing.

These tips work on Samsung, Xiaomi/Redmi, Oppo, Vivo, Infinix, Tecno, Realme, and any other Android brand.

Why Do Android Phones Slow Down?

It helps to know the enemy. Phones slow down because of:

  • Full storage — Android needs free space to work smoothly; below ~10% free, everything crawls.
  • Too many background apps — apps you “closed” are often still running, eating RAM.
  • Bloated apps and cache — months of cached data pile up.
  • Old software — missed updates mean missed performance fixes.
  • A dying battery — on some phones, a worn battery causes the system to throttle performance.
  • Too many widgets and animations — pretty, but they cost resources.

Now, the fixes — starting with the ones that make the biggest difference.

Tip 1: Free Up Storage (The #1 Fix)

This single step fixes more slow phones than anything else. Aim to keep at least 15–20% of your storage free.

  1. Go to Settings → Storage to see what’s eating space.
  2. Delete what you don’t need:
  3. Old WhatsApp videos and forwarded memes (often gigabytes!) — check WhatsApp → Settings → Storage and Data → Manage Storage.
  4. Downloaded movies or dramas you’ve already watched.
  5. Duplicate photos and blurry shots.
  6. Old APK files in your Downloads folder.
  7. Move photos/videos to Google Photos (free backup) or a memory card, then delete the local copies.
  8. Uninstall apps you haven’t opened in months — be honest with yourself.

On many Pakistani users’ phones, WhatsApp media alone frees up 5–10 GB. Do this first.

Tip 2: Clear App Cache (Not App Data!)

Cache is temporary data apps store to load faster — but it grows forever and gets corrupted.

  1. Go to Settings → Apps → pick a heavy app (Facebook, TikTok, Chrome, Instagram).
  2. Tap Storage → Clear Cache.

Important: “Clear Cache” is safe. “Clear Data” logs you out and deletes the app’s saved information — don’t tap that unless you mean to.

For a quicker sweep, use Settings → Storage → Cached data (on some phones) or the built-in cleaner app that Xiaomi, Oppo, and Vivo include.

Tip 3: Uninstall or Disable Bloatware

Budget phones in Pakistan (Infinix, Tecno, Xiaomi) come stuffed with pre-installed apps you’ll never use — duplicate browsers, “cleaner” apps, games, shopping apps.

  1. Go to Settings → Apps and scroll through the full list.
  2. For apps you can’t uninstall, tap Disable — this stops them from running and hides them.
  3. Say no to third-party “RAM booster” / “phone cleaner” apps from the Play Store — most are ad-filled junk that make things worse. Your phone already manages RAM itself.

Tip 4: Reduce Background Activity

Apps running in the background drain RAM and battery.

  1. Go to Settings → Apps → tap an app → Battery → set to Restricted (or “Optimize”) for apps you don’t need live notifications from — like games or shopping apps.
  2. Keep notifications on only for messaging, email, and banking apps.
  3. On Samsung: use Device Care → Battery → Background usage limits to put unused apps to sleep automatically.

Tip 5: Turn Off Unnecessary Animations

Animations make Android feel fancy but slow down older phones. Reducing them makes everything feel snappier instantly.

  1. Enable Developer Options: Settings → About Phone → tap “Build Number” 7 times.
  2. Go to Settings → System → Developer Options.
  3. Set Window animation scale, Transition animation scale, and Animator duration scale to 0.5x (or off).

Your phone will feel noticeably faster within seconds. This is safe and reversible.

Tip 6: Update Your Software

Updates aren’t just new features — they include performance improvements and bug fixes.

  1. Settings → Software Update → download and install any pending update.
  2. Update your apps too: Play Store → profile icon → Manage apps → Update all.
  3. If your phone no longer gets updates (common after 2–3 years on budget phones), at least keep your apps and browser current.

Tip 7: Restart Your Phone Weekly

It sounds too simple, but a restart clears stuck processes, frees RAM, and fixes mysterious slowdowns. If you haven’t restarted in weeks, do it now. Make it a weekly habit — e.g., every Friday.

Tip 8: Use Lite Versions of Heavy Apps

Facebook, Messenger, and TikTok are notorious resource hogs.

  • Switch to Facebook Lite and Messenger Lite — official, much lighter, and work better on slow networks too.
  • Use YouTube Go or just watch in your browser if the app lags.
  • Access heavy sites (like Facebook) through Chrome instead of the app — one less app running in the background.

Tip 9: Clean Up Your Home Screen

Every widget on your home screen is a mini-app running constantly.

  • Remove widgets you don’t actually check (weather, news, clocks you never look at).
  • Use a static wallpaper instead of a live wallpaper.
  • Keep your home screen to 1–2 pages — fewer icons to load.

Tip 10: Check for Malware

A sudden, severe slowdown — especially with pop-up ads appearing on your home screen or in notifications — can mean adware or malware, often from apps installed outside the Play Store.

  1. Uninstall any app you don’t remember installing, especially “free” games or “video downloader” apps from outside the Play Store.
  2. Run Play Protect: Play Store → profile icon → Play Protect → Scan.
  3. Avoid installing APKs from random websites and WhatsApp forwards — that’s the #1 malware source in Pakistan.

Tip 11: Factory Reset (The Nuclear Option)

If you’ve tried everything and the phone is still painfully slow, a factory reset gives you a fresh start.

  1. Back up first! Photos → Google Photos; WhatsApp → Settings → Chats → Chat Backup; contacts sync to your Google account automatically.
  2. Go to Settings → System → Reset → Erase all data (factory reset).
  3. After the reset, don’t restore every old app — reinstall only what you actually use. (Restoring everything often restores the slowness too.)

This genuinely makes old phones feel new again — as long as the hardware itself isn’t failing.

Tip 12: Know When It’s Really the Hardware

Software fixes can’t fix everything. Consider replacing the phone (or at least the battery) if:

  • The phone is 4+ years old and struggles even after a factory reset.
  • The battery drains in a few hours or the phone shuts off at 20%.
  • Apps you need for work/school no longer support your Android version.
  • The phone overheats during basic use.

For a battery replacement, official service centres are safest — a fresh battery on an otherwise fine phone can add a year or two of life for a fraction of a new phone’s price.

Quick-Reference Checklist

Do these in order, and stop when your phone feels fast enough:

  1. ☐ Free up storage to 15–20% free (check WhatsApp media!)
  2. ☐ Clear cache of heavy apps
  3. ☐ Uninstall/disable bloatware and unused apps
  4. ☐ Restrict background battery usage for non-essential apps
  5. ☐ Reduce animations to 0.5x
  6. ☐ Install system + app updates
  7. ☐ Restart the phone
  8. ☐ Switch to Lite apps
  9. ☐ Trim widgets and live wallpapers
  10. ☐ Scan for malware with Play Protect

FAQ

Will clearing RAM with a “booster” app speed up my phone? No — Android manages RAM automatically, and force-closing apps often makes the phone slower because reopening them costs more resources. Uninstall booster apps; they’re usually adware.

How much free storage does my phone need? Keep at least 10% free as an absolute minimum; 15–20% is the comfort zone where Android runs smoothly.

Does a factory reset delete my WhatsApp chats? Yes, unless you back them up first. Go to WhatsApp → Settings → Chats → Chat Backup and back up to Google Drive before resetting.

Why is my phone slow even though storage is half empty? Likely culprits: too many background apps, an app misbehaving after a bad update, or an ageing battery causing throttling. Work through tips 4–7, and check tip 10 for malware.

Are “phone cleaner” apps safe? The built-in cleaner from your phone’s manufacturer (Xiaomi, Samsung Device Care, etc.) is fine. Random cleaner apps from the Play Store are mostly ad-filled and unnecessary — avoid them.

Conclusion

You don’t need a new phone — you need a cleaner phone. For most people, freeing up storage, clearing cache, removing bloatware, and toning down animations will make a 2–3 year old Android feel dramatically faster, and the whole process takes under an hour.

Do the big cleanup once, then keep the weekly restart habit and an eye on WhatsApp storage. Your phone (and your wallet) will thank you.
